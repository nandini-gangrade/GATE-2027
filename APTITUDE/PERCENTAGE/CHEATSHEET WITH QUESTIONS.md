# 0️⃣ BASIC CONCEPT: What does percentage mean?

### 📌 Formula: Calculate the percentage of a quantity

$$
\boxed{\text{Percentage}=\frac{\text{Part}}{\text{Whole}}\times100}
$$

**Use when:** You know a part and the total and need to express the part as a percentage of the total.

### Example

Out of 200 students, 50 passed.

$$
\text{Pass percentage}=\frac{50}{200}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{25\%}
$$

🧠 **Shortcut:** Always ask:

> "What is the part, and what is the total?"

The total is the denominator.

⚠️ **Trap:** If 50 is 25% of 200, then 200 is not 25% of 50.

# 1️⃣ CALCULATE THE VALUE FROM A GIVEN PERCENTAGE

### 📌 Formula: Find the part when the percentage and total are known

$$
\boxed{\text{Part}=\frac{\text{Percentage}}{100}\times\text{Total}}
$$

**Use when:** A question asks for a particular percentage of a given number.

### Example 1: Basic

Find 20% of 600.

$$
\text{Part}=\frac{20}{100}\times600
$$

$$
=\boxed{120}
$$

### 🧠 Shortcut: Convert the percentage to a fraction

$$
20\%=\frac15
$$

Therefore:

$$
600\div5=\boxed{120}
$$

### Example 2: GATE-style

Find 12.5% of 640.

$$
12.5\%=\frac18
$$

$$
640\div8=\boxed{80}
$$

### Example 3: Percentage of a percentage

Find 20% of 30% of 500.

$$
\frac{20}{100}\times\frac{30}{100}\times500
$$

$$
=\frac15\times\frac3{10}\times500
$$

$$
=\boxed{30}
$$

### 🧠 Shortcut

> **"Of" means multiply.**

$$
20\%\text{ of }30\%
$$

means:

$$
20\%\times30\%
$$

⚠️ **Trap:** 20% of 30 is 6, not 50.

## 🔥 Quick method to find the numerator

### Question: 62.5% = ?

You know:

$$
\frac18=12.5\%
$$

$$
\text{Numerator}=\frac{62.5}{12.5}=5
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{62.5\%=\frac58}
$$

### General formula

$$
\boxed{\text{Numerator}=\frac{\text{Given percentage}}{\text{Base percentage}}}
$$

Use this only when the denominator is already known.

### 📌 Formula: Find the remaining percentage

$$
\boxed{\text{Remaining percentage}=100\%-\text{Given percentage}}
$$

**Use when:** A question asks about the remaining portion or the fraction left.

### Example

If 35% of students are absent, what percentage is present?

$$
100-35=\boxed{65\%}
$$

### 🧠 Shortcut

# 4️⃣ PERCENTAGE AS A MULTIPLIER

This is one of the most useful concepts in GATE aptitude.

## 📌 Formula: Increase multiplier

$$
\boxed{\text{Increase multiplier}=1+\frac{x}{100}}
$$

**Use when:** A value increases by \(x\%\).

### Examples

| Increase |                Multiplier |
| -------: | ------------------------: |
|      10% |     \(1+0.1=\boxed{1.1}\) |
|      20% |     \(1+0.2=\boxed{1.2}\) |
|      36% |   \(1+0.36=\boxed{1.36}\) |
|       4% |   \(1+0.04=\boxed{1.04}\) |
|    12.5% | \(1+0.125=\boxed{1.125}\) |

### Example

A salary of ₹1,000 increases by 20%.

$$
\text{New}=1000\times1.2
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1,200}
$$

---

## 📌 Formula: Decrease multiplier

$$
\boxed{\text{Decrease multiplier}=1-\frac{x}{100}}
$$

**Use when:** A value decreases by \(x\%\).

| Decrease |                Multiplier |
| -------: | ------------------------: |
|      10% |     \(1-0.1=\boxed{0.9}\) |
|      20% |     \(1-0.2=\boxed{0.8}\) |
|      36% |   \(1-0.36=\boxed{0.64}\) |
|       4% |   \(1-0.04=\boxed{0.96}\) |
|    12.5% | \(1-0.125=\boxed{0.875}\) |

### Example

A machine produces 600 units. Its output decreases by 20%.

$$
\text{New}=600\times0.8
$$

$$
\boxed{480\text{ units}}
$$

# 5️⃣ MULTIPLIER ↔ PERCENTAGE CHANGE

## 📌 Formula: Convert a multiplier into percentage change

### If multiplier > 1:

$$
\boxed{\%\text{ increase}=(M-1)\times100}
$$

### If multiplier < 1:

$$
\boxed{\%\text{ decrease}=(1-M)\times100}
$$

**Use when:** A question says that a value becomes \(M\) times its original value.

### Example 1

A value becomes 1.36 times its original value.

$$
(1.36-1)\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{36\%\text{ increase}}
$$

### Example 2

A value becomes 0.64 times its original value.

$$
(1-0.64)\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{36\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

### 🧠 Remember

| Multiplier | Meaning        |
| ---------: | -------------- |
|        1.5 | 50% increase   |
|       1.25 | 25% increase   |
|      1.125 | 12.5% increase |
|          1 | No change      |
|      0.875 | 12.5% decrease |
|        0.8 | 20% decrease   |
|       0.64 | 36% decrease   |

# 6️⃣ SPECIAL FRACTION–MULTIPLIER TRICK

You mentioned this important concept:

> 12.5% = 1/8, so 112.5% = 9/8.

Let's formalize it.

## 📌 Formula: Convert an increase percentage into a fractional multiplier

$$
\boxed{
1+\frac{x}{100}
=
\frac{\text{Denominator}+\text{Numerator}}{\text{Denominator}}
}
$$

**Use when:** You know the percentage increase as a fraction and want a quick multiplier.

### Example 1: 12.5% increase

$$
12.5\%=\frac18
$$

Therefore:

$$
1+\frac18
=
\frac88+\frac18
=
\boxed{\frac98}
$$

So:

$$
112.5\%=\frac98=1.125
$$

### Example 2: 25% increase

$$
25\%=\frac14
$$

Therefore:

$$
1+\frac14
=
\frac44+\frac14
=
\boxed{\frac54}
$$

So:

$$
125\%=\frac54=1.25
$$

### Example 3: 20% increase

$$
20\%=\frac15
$$

$$
1+\frac15
=
\frac55+\frac15
=
\boxed{\frac65}
$$

So:

$$
120\%=\frac65=1.2
$$

### 🧠 Shortcut

> **Increase → Add the original fraction to 1.**
>
> **Decrease → Subtract the fraction from 1.**

For example:

$$
1-\frac18=\frac78
$$

Therefore, a **12.5% decrease leaves 87.5% = 7/8**.

# 7️⃣ PERCENTAGE CHANGE: INCREASE AND DECREASE

## 📌 Formula: Percentage increase

$$
\boxed{
\%\text{ increase}
=
\frac{\text{Final}-\text{Initial}}{\text{Initial}}\times100
}
$$

**Use when:** A value increases from an initial value to a final value.

### Example

A price increases from ₹500 to ₹600.

$$
\frac{600-500}{500}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{20\%\text{ increase}}
$$

---

## 📌 Formula: Percentage decrease

$$
\boxed{
\%\text{ decrease}
=
\frac{\text{Initial}-\text{Final}}{\text{Initial}}\times100
}
$$

**Use when:** A value decreases from an initial value to a final value.

### Example

A price decreases from ₹600 to ₹480.

$$
\frac{600-480}{600}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{20\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

### ⚠️ GATE trap

The denominator is the **initial/reference value**, not the final value.

---

# 8️⃣ PERCENTAGE DIFFERENCE

## 📌 Formula: Percentage difference between two values

$$
\boxed{
\%\text{ difference}
=
\frac{|\text{Value}_1-\text{Value}_2|}
{\frac{\text{Value}_1+\text{Value}_2}{2}}
\times100
}
$$

**Use when:** You are comparing two values without designating either one as the original or reference.

### Example

Two measured values are 80 and 100.

$$
\%\text{ difference}
=
\frac{|100-80|}{(100+80)/2}\times100
$$

$$
=\frac{20}{90}\times100
$$

$$
\boxed{22.22\%}
$$

⚠️ **Important distinction:**

* Percentage change uses the **initial/reference value**.
* Percentage difference uses the **average of the two values**.

If a question says "percentage difference" but clearly specifies a reference value, read the wording carefully.

## 📌 Formula: Interpret a multiplier

$$

**Use when:** A value is multiplied by a decimal and you need to identify the percentage change.

$$

$$
1-0.7=0.3
$$

$$
\boxed{30\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

### Example 2

A value becomes 1.2 times its original value.

$$
1.2-1=0.2
$$

$$
\boxed{20\%\text{ increase}}
$$

### Example 3

A value becomes 0.956 times its original value.

$$
1-0.956=0.044
$$

$$
0.044\times100
=
\boxed{4.4\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

# 🔟 SUCCESSIVE PERCENTAGE CHANGES

This is an important GATE aptitude topic.

## 📌 Method 1: Multiply the multipliers

$$
\boxed{
\text{Final value}
=
\text{Initial value}
\times M_1\times M_2\times\cdots
}
$$

**Use when:** A value changes more than once.

### Example

A number increases by 10%, then increases by 20%, then decreases by 30%.

### Step 1: Convert each change into a multiplier

$$
10\%\text{ increase}=1.1
$$

$$
20\%\text{ increase}=1.2
$$

$$
30\%\text{ decrease}=0.7
$$

### Step 2: Multiply

$$
1.1\times1.2\times0.7=0.924
$$

So the final value is 92.4% of the original.

$$
\boxed{7.6\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

### 🧠 Trick

> Multiply the multipliers first. Interpret the final multiplier at the end.

---

## 📌 Method 2: Two successive changes shortcut

$$
\boxed{
\text{Net change}
=
a+b+\frac{ab}{100}
}
$$

**Use when:** There are exactly two successive percentage changes.

Use positive signs for increases and negative signs for decreases.

### Example 1: Two increases

Increase by 10%, then 20%.

$$
10+20+\frac{10\times20}{100}
$$

$$
=30+2
$$

$$
\boxed{32\%\text{ increase}}
$$

### Example 2: Increase, then decrease

Increase by 20%, then decrease by 10%.

$$
20+(-10)+\frac{20(-10)}{100}
$$

$$
=20-10-2
$$

$$
\boxed{8\%\text{ increase}}
$$

### Example 3: Two decreases

Decrease by 10%, then 20%.

$$
-10-20+\frac{(-10)(-20)}{100}
$$

$$
=-30+2
$$

$$
\boxed{28\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

---

## ⭐ Special shortcut: Equal increase and decrease

### 📌 Formula

If a value increases by \(x\%\) and then decreases by \(x\%\):

$$
\boxed{
\text{Net loss}=\frac{x^2}{100}\%
}
$$

**Use when:** The same percentage increase and decrease happen successively.

### Example

Increase by 20%, then decrease by 20%.

$$
\frac{20^2}{100}=4\%
$$

$$
\boxed{4\%\text{ decrease}}
$$

### Why?

$$
1.2\times0.8=0.96
$$

Only 96% remains.

# 1️⃣2️⃣ MORE THAN ↔ LESS THAN

This is a common source of mistakes.

## 📌 Formula: Convert "A is x% more than B" into "B is what % less than A?"

$$
\boxed{
\%\text{ less}
=
\frac{x}{100+x}\times100
}
$$

**Use when:** The reference changes from B to A.

### Example

A is 25% more than B. Find how much less B is than A.

Assume:

$$
B=100
$$

Then:

$$
A=125
$$

Now calculate the decrease from A to B:

$$
\frac{125-100}{125}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{20\%}
$$

Therefore:

> A is 25% more than B, but B is 20% less than A.

---

## 📌 Formula: Convert "A is x% less than B" into "B is what % more than A?"

$$
\boxed{
\%\text{ more}
=
\frac{x}{100-x}\times100
}
$$

### Example

A is 20% less than B.

Assume:

$$
B=100
$$

Then:

$$
A=80
$$

Now:

$$
\frac{100-80}{80}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{25\%}
$$

Therefore:

> A is 20% less than B, but B is 25% more than A.

## 📌 Formula: Convert a ratio into percentages

If:

$$
A:B=m:n
$$

Then:

$$
\boxed{

$$

$$

$$

Boys' percentage:

$$

=
\boxed{60\%}
$$

$$
\frac25\times100
=
\boxed{40\%}
$$

### 🧠 Trick

> **Ratio parts ÷ total parts × 100**

# 1️⃣8️⃣ POPULATION / SALARY / PRODUCTION GROWTH

These are real-world applications of successive percentage change.

## 📌 Formula: Repeated percentage increase

$$
\boxed{
\text{Final value}
=
\text{Initial value}
\left(1+\frac{r}{100}\right)^n
}
$$

**Use when:** A value increases by the same percentage every year or every period.

* \(r\) = percentage growth per period
* \(n\) = number of periods

### Example

A population of 1,000 grows by 10% annually for 2 years.

$$
\text{Final}=1000(1.1)^2
$$

$$
=1000\times1.21
$$

$$
\boxed{1210}
$$

### 🧠 Trick

Repeated percentage changes are **multiplicative**, not additive.

❌ 10% + 10% = 20%

✅ Actual increase = 21%.

# 1️⃣9️⃣ REPEATED PERCENTAGE DECREASE

## 📌 Formula

$$
\boxed{
\text{Final value}
=
\text{Initial value}
\left(1-\frac{r}{100}\right)^n
}
$$

### Example

A machine's value is ₹10,000. It depreciates by 10% every year for 2 years.

$$
10000(0.9)^2
$$

$$
=10000\times0.81
$$

$$
\boxed{₹8,100}
$$

Overall decrease:

$$
100-81=\boxed{19\%}
$$

# 2️⃣0️⃣ REVERSE SUCCESSIVE CHANGES

### GATE-style question

A number increases by 20% and then decreases by 20%. By what percentage should the final value increase to return to the original?

### Step 1: Find the final multiplier

$$
1.2\times0.8=0.96
$$

So the final value is 96% of the original.

### Step 2: Find the required increase

Suppose the original is 100.

Final = 96.

Required increase:

$$
\frac{100-96}{96}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{4.1667\%}
$$

⚠️ **Trap:** The required reverse increase is not 4%. The reference value is now 96.

# 2️⃣1️⃣ PERCENTAGE ERROR

This is especially relevant to GATE Engineering Mathematics and numerical questions.

## 📌 Formula: Percentage error

$$
\boxed{
\%\text{ error}
=
\frac{|\text{Measured}-\text{Actual}|}
{\text{Actual}}\times100
}
$$

**Use when:** Comparing a measured/estimated value with the accepted or actual value.

### Example

Actual value = 200.

Measured value = 190.

$$
\frac{|190-200|}{200}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{5\%}
$$

### ⚠️ Important

Percentage error and percentage difference are not necessarily the same.

# 2️⃣2️⃣ PERCENTAGE ERROR IN PRODUCTS AND POWERS

Useful for GATE Engineering Mathematics.

If:

$$
Z=A^n
$$

Then for small relative errors:

$$
\boxed{
\frac{\Delta Z}{Z}
\approx
n\frac{\Delta A}{A}
}
$$

In percentage form:

$$
\boxed{
\%\text{ error in }Z
\approx
n\times\%\text{ error in }A
}
$$

### Example

The radius of a circle has a 2% measurement error. Find the approximate percentage error in its area.

$$
A=\pi r^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\%\text{ error in area}
\approx2\times2
$$

$$
\boxed{4\%}
$$

⚠️ This is an approximation for small errors.

# 2️⃣3️⃣ DISCOUNT AND MARKED PRICE

This is an important application of percentages.

## 📌 Formula: Selling price after discount

$$
\boxed{
\text{Selling Price}
=
\text{Marked Price}
\left(1-\frac{\text{Discount \%}}{100}\right)
}
$$

**Use when:** A discount is applied to a marked price.

### Example

Marked price = ₹2,000.

Discount = 25%.

$$
SP=2000(1-0.25)
$$

$$
=2000\times0.75
$$

$$
\boxed{₹1,500}
$$

---

## 📌 Formula: Successive discounts

$$
\boxed{
\text{Final price}
=
MP(1-d_1)(1-d_2)
}
$$

where \(d_1,d_2\) are decimal discount rates.

### Example

Two successive discounts of 20% and 10%:

$$
0.8\times0.9=0.72
$$

So 72% of the marked price remains.

Total discount:

$$
100-72=\boxed{28\%}
$$

# 2️⃣4️⃣ PROFIT AND LOSS

Another common percentage application.

## 📌 Formula: Profit percentage

$$
\boxed{
\text{Profit \%}
=
\frac{SP-CP}{CP}\times100
}
$$

**Use when:** Selling price is greater than cost price.

## 📌 Formula: Loss percentage

$$
\boxed{
\text{Loss \%}
=
\frac{CP-SP}{CP}\times100
}
$$

**Use when:** Selling price is less than cost price.

### Example

Cost price = ₹800.

Selling price = ₹1,000.

$$
\text{Profit \%}
=
\frac{1000-800}{800}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{25\%}
$$

⚠️ **Trap:** Profit percentage is calculated on cost price, not selling price.

# 2️⃣5️⃣ INCOME, EXPENDITURE AND SAVINGS

### GATE-style question

A person's income increases by 20%, while expenditure increases by 10%. Initially, income = ₹10,000 and expenditure = ₹8,000. Find the percentage change in savings.

### Step 1: Initial savings

$$
10000-8000=2000
$$

### Step 2: New income

$$
10000\times1.2=12000
$$

### Step 3: New expenditure

$$
8000\times1.1=8800
$$

### Step 4: New savings

$$
12000-8800=3200
$$

### Step 5: Percentage increase in savings

$$
\frac{3200-2000}{2000}\times100
$$

$$
=\boxed{60\%\text{ increase}}
$$

⚠️ **Trap:** You cannot directly subtract the income and expenditure percentages to find the savings percentage.

# 2️⃣6️⃣ TIME–SPEED–DISTANCE PERCENTAGE TRICKS

Useful for GATE aptitude.

## 📌 Formula: If distance is constant

$$
\boxed{
\text{Speed}\times\text{Time}=\text{Constant}
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
S_1T_1=S_2T_2
}
$$

### Example

Speed increases by 25%. Find the percentage change in time for the same distance.

New speed multiplier:

$$
1.25
$$

New time multiplier:

$$
\frac1{1.25}=0.8
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{20\%\text{ decrease in time}}
$$

### 🧠 Shortcut

If speed increases by \(x\%\):

$$
\boxed{
\text{Time decrease}
=
\frac{x}{100+x}\times100
}
$$

For 25%:

$$
\frac{25}{125}\times100=20\%
$$

### GATE-style question

A person's efficiency increases by 20%. By what percentage does the time required to complete the same work decrease?

Efficiency and time are inversely proportional.

$$
\text{New time multiplier}=\frac1{1.2}
$$

$$
=\frac56
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{16.67\%\text{ decrease}}

# 2️⃣8️⃣ MIXTURE / COMPOSITION PERCENTAGES

### 📌 Formula: Percentage of a component

$$
\boxed{
\%\text{ of component}
=
\frac{\text{Component quantity}}{\text{Total mixture quantity}}\times100
}
$$

### Example

A 200 g mixture contains 50 g of salt.

$$
\frac{50}{200}\times100
=
\boxed{25\%}
$$

### ⚠️ Trap

When adding another substance, the total mixture changes, so the percentage may change even if the original quantity remains the same.
